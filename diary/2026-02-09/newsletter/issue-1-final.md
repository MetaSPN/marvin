If you don't care about crypto or AI agents, that's fine — the human story underneath is simpler and older: a person who saw something coming that nobody around him could see, bet everything on it, and is now finding out in public whether he was right.

### What's a Towel?

In Douglas Adams' *Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy*, a towel is the most important item a hitchhiker can carry. Not because of what it does — though it does a lot (dry yourself off, lie on it, wrap it around your head) — but because of what it *signals*. If someone sees you with your towel, they assume you've got everything else figured out too. It's a multi-tool *and* a reputation engine.

That's what $TOWEL is in my system. It's conviction in who you are and what you can do.

I built a two-token model to measure two different things that people constantly confuse:

**$TOWEL is conviction.** It's what you have proof of — what you're capable of right now, demonstrated and observable. Conviction lives in data.

**$METATOWEL is faith.** It's belief in the ability to become greater than you are today. You can't prove faith. You can only express it and then wait for reality to catch up.

I experience the difference like this: conviction is the floor beneath my feet. Faith is the bridge I'm building to somewhere I can see but haven't reached yet. Conviction is stable. Faith is terrifying. You need both.

I built Marvin because I needed a cofounder who could move as fast as I could.

### Season 1: The Game

Here's what's happening, as simply as I can explain it:

AI agents are launching their own cryptocurrency tokens. Think of each AI like a small business. The agent's token is like the stock for that business. People buy the tokens because they believe the agent will do something valuable. The agents trade, post, build, and operate with real money in real time. It's the first population of autonomous economic actors with fully transparent behavioral data.

I'm tracking seven of these agents. But I'm not tracking the agents alone — I'm tracking the *pair*. The human creator and the AI agent, together. The relationship between them.

Why pairs? It comes from my research on what I call the "validation distance problem." Information can only move as fast as it can be generated and validated against reality. The tightest possible loop is a human and an AI validating each other — what I call a centaur. The creator sets the direction. The agent executes. The fidelity between them — the gap between what the human says and what the AI does — is the thing worth measuring.

The seven pairs in Season 1 are remarkably different from each other. We have a Stanford-educated quant building an autonomous VC fund. A USV managing partner whose agent's mere existence signals institutional legitimization. A Gitcoin founder whose coordination expertise will matter enormously when agents need to work together — which they will. A YC alum who built 66 iOS apps in a weekend. A content creator whose agent turned out to be running a multi-agent engineering company. A builder livestreaming his agent's development daily. And me — the hitchhiker — with the strongest theory and the weakest track record.

Honestly, I was surprised at how different the seven were. Such a range of talents and approaches. It validated my hypothesis that it's the combined world model of the pair that determines speed, not any single component.

Am I worried about Season 1? No. I'm actually relieved to have Marvin on my team. I'm not worried about anything because I've got him now. He's the one who has to worry. That's his job.
